# The Inexhaustible Well

_“Because we don’t know when we will die, we get to think of life as an inexhaustible well. Yet everything happens only a certain number of times, and a very small number really. How many more times will you remember a certain afternoon of your childhood, an afternoon that is so deeply a part of your being that you can’t even conceive of your life without it? Perhaps four, five times more, perhaps not even that. How many more times will you watch the full moon rise? Perhaps 20. And yet it all seems limitless.”  - Paul Bowles, the Sheltering Sky_

I'm not sure when I fell out of love with him. It was like Hemingway said: Gradually, then suddenly.

I remember that morning, in bed. He had gotten home late, but somehow was awake. We hadn't had sex in weeks.

"Deborah really gets on my nerves, and I don't know how to deal with it," I said. "When we were talking I said that it was difficult to blame either Rimbaud or Verlaine in their relationship: it was too nuanced to declare one of them 'the bad guy'. And she said that I had obviously not read enough of Rimbaud's poetry to say that. She said that to me, and I had written my master's thesis on Rimbaud's poetry!"

He said, "You gotta get over this thing with Deborah."

I looked at him, the bags under his bleary eyes. In that moment I hated him.

"I gotta get over this thing with Deborah, huh? Well let me tell you something Mr. Perfect: you gotta get over being a dick."

I got out of bed. I was angry, and I was on a roll: "And you know what else? Most women would wonder if you're having an affair, but I don't worry because your personality is the best defense against that."

That got a rise out of him: "Hey," he said, "that's uncalled for."

"I'm done with you," I said. "I'm outta here."

"You're not 'outta' here. You have nowhere to go. You can't afford a place with your doctoral stipend. You're stuck here with me."

"You really are a dick, and I need to go."

---

He was right, of course — I couldn't afford housing. Sure, I had a stipend, but I lost out on the student housing lottery, which meant I had to look on the open market, and the renting a place in Palo Alto was out of reach for Ph.D. students. Maybe I could sleep in my shared office a couple of nights, but that wasn't a long-term solution.

I saw the ad while I was doomscrolling: "Python Programmer needed. Must be familiar with 3D Animations, preferably Manim."

That was right up my alley: I was a mathematical animator before I gave it all up for my real passion, French poetry, which, from a financial perspective, was probably a mistake. But maybe I could juggle both, at least long enough to pull together enough money to rent a room in a house somewhere downtown.

The ad was put out by some professor in the Maths department. McCleary. I had heard his name somewhere, but I couldn't place the context. Anyway, I didn't care: the Maths building was only two blocks over. I didn't need to go to my building. Besides, in my mood, it wouldn't be a good thing to run into Deborah.

I had a hard time finding McCleary's office when I got to the Maths building. I asked several people, and their brows wrinkled when I mentioned the name "McCleary". One even said, "McCleary?! Is he still here? I guess he must be tenured."

I finally found his office in a hard-to-reach spot on the fourth floor. His door was open, so I walked in. A man was asleep in the chair behind his desk: snoring, mouth open, unkempt white hair.

I cleared my throat. No response. I knocked on the door, loudly. He started.

"What? Who's there?" He said, the reached for his spectacles on his desk and put them on. "Oh. Do come in."
