# The Inexhaustible Well

_“Because we don’t know when we will die, we get to think of life as an inexhaustible well. Yet everything happens only a certain number of times, and a very small number really. How many more times will you remember a certain afternoon of your childhood, an afternoon that is so deeply a part of your being that you can’t even conceive of your life without it? Perhaps four, five times more, perhaps not even that. How many more times will you watch the full moon rise? Perhaps 20. And yet it all seems limitless.”  - Paul Bowles, the Sheltering Sky_

I'm not sure when I fell out of love with him. It was like Hemingway said: Gradually, then suddenly.

I remember that morning, in bed. He had gotten home late, but somehow was awake. We hadn't had sex in weeks even though we shared the same bed.

"Deborah really gets on my nerves, and I don't know how to deal with it," I said. "When we were talking I said that it was difficult to blame either Rimbaud or Verlaine in their relationship: it was too nuanced to declare one of them 'the bad guy'. And she said that I had obviously not read enough of Rimbaud's poetry to say that. She said that to me, and I had written my thesis on Rimbaud's poetry!"

He said, "You gotta get over this thing with Deborah."

I looked at him, saw the bags under his bleary eyes. In that moment I hated him.
